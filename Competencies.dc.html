How to use this framework

Reading the scale

Meets expectations

Meeting expectations is successful performance. It includes dependable follow-through, respectful conduct, appropriate anticipation and required safeguards. These are not reserved for exceptional performers. Apply the baseline to the role and agreed stage of development, including appropriate supervision.

Exceeds expectations

Exceeding expectations builds on that baseline. Look for sustained evidence of stronger judgement, anticipation or handling of complexity relative to the role. Routine anticipation or a normal duty does not become exceptional merely because it happened without prompting. Larger scope is not required: a precise decision in a difficult situation within the person’s own remit can qualify.

Scope and evidence, for every competency

Assess only contexts the role can reasonably influence. Organisational examples apply to people with the corresponding decision responsibility. Examples of exceeding expectations qualify only where they go beyond the role’s normal baseline.

Evidence: observed actions, explanations of decisions, relevant work records and specific feedback.
